# Birb Hangout — a shared place for distant friends to spend time together

## What was built and why

Birb Hangout is a browser-based 3D social world built for friends and family who live far apart. Remote connection does not always need another feed, agenda, competitive loop, or text channel. Sometimes people want the digital equivalent of sharing a room—reading, choosing music, watching something, playing a game, resting by the fire, or setting off together. The product turns the idea into an inhabitable lounge where each person appears as a bird and the reward is the company itself.

That goal shaped the interface. Activities remain visible in the world instead of replacing it with full-screen menus. A bird browses the record cabinet, carries a sleeve to the turntable, and puts it on. A reader holds an open book while another bird remains visible across the room. The television, sofa, fireplace cushions, bookshelf, photo frame, wall switches, and trivia screen are spatial objects others can notice and approach. Focused overlays exist for setup—typing a video, selecting a title, answering a question—but the activity returns to the room. The result is not vaguely “multiplayer play.” It is low-pressure co-presence: people share a setting while choosing different levels of attention and participation.

The implemented product has grown far beyond the repository’s old scaffold description. It includes invite-code rooms; persistent room identity; customizable avatars; walking, seating, sleeping, emotes, and consent-based hugs; shared video and record playback; a room-owned vinyl cabinet and bookshelf; PDF and EPUB reading; voice chat; photo uploads; lighting and fireplace ambience; trivia with a synchronized physical ceremony; and a Dream reached from fireside rest. In the Dream, players fly through the same seeded landscape, follow one another, cross floating islands, and descend into rivers, clearings, and trees. Alongside the game, the repository contains a serious development environment: specialist studios, exact-time capture tools, spatial annotation, blind comparison packets, negative controls, candidate state machines, change ledgers, and visual-evidence gates.

Distant friends and family receive more ways to be together than either “talk continuously” or “play the same competitive game.” Quiet and active moods coexist. One person can read while another explores; a third can join the sofa, ask for a hug, or settle by the fire. Shared objects make intention legible without forcing it through chat. The project also shows 3D teams how visual feedback can be grounded in a reproducible camera, object, world coordinate, and scene state rather than conveyed as an ambiguous sentence about “the thing near the left edge.”

## The end-to-end experience

A visitor creates a private room, chooses a name and bird appearance, and receives a short code embedded in the room URL. A friend opens that URL and joins the same Colyseus room. The code is more than a lobby label: it keys persistent room state, while a locally stored owner token allows the creator to reclaim host authority without requiring accounts. Fast refresh and reconnect use a stable browser client identity so a returning person reclaims the same live bird instead of appearing twice. A typed code that does not exist is rejected rather than silently creating an empty duplicate—an important response to the otherwise confusing failure mode where two friends believe they joined the same room but cannot see each other.

Inside, people navigate a furnished Three.js lounge and interact with spatial targets. Seating is authoritative and exclusive, so two players cannot occupy the same chair. The shared snapshot carries presence, position, pose, seat, avatar, room lighting, photo slides, vinyl records, shelf contents, media state, fireplace state, game state, and Dream membership. Local concerns such as camera mode and the private breathing interaction remain local. This distinction keeps the room coherent without synchronizing every transient UI choice.

The activities are implemented as visible sequences. Choosing music can begin at a shared cabinet, continue as a carried sleeve and wing animation, and resolve at the turntable. The cabinet belongs to the room rather than one browser’s local storage. The sleeve can derive its thumbnail and an accent color sampled from image pixels, so the physical prop reflects the selected media. Watching uses exactly one embedded video player shared by the TV and turntable paths. The server holds the selected video, status, shared playhead, duration, and a host synchronization token. One bird can temporarily hold the physical remote; everyone else follows the same state instead of issuing racing commands. Clients correct meaningful drift, reject stale player clocks, and re-anchor startup playback to a real reported playhead.

Reading solves two different rendering problems instead of compromising between them. The local reader needs crisp controllable pages; everyone else needs a believable bird, book, and pose in the world. A close reading view renders PDF pages and a deliberately bounded EPUB 2/3 subset onto textures. The EPUB path walks the container, package manifest, and spine, performs its own line wrapping and pagination, and maintains a ten-texture cache. Fixed-layout EPUBs are refused explicitly rather than rendered incorrectly. In the shared scene, a separate book actor stays attached to the bird’s pose. The page-turn animation deforms a 25-by-17 vertex sheet, maps the following page correctly on the reverse, and avoids the common shortcut of rotating a rigid card or floating a second texture plane above the paper. Players can curate which of nine built-in books appear as physical shelf spines, and each reader advances privately while remaining visibly present to the room.

Social play spans quick reactions and coordinated events. The radial emote control is anchored to the local bird and supports hold, direction, and release rather than becoming another permanent panel. A hug is a separate interaction rather than just another emote: the initiator approaches a nearby bird, sees a short local offer, sends a private request, and the recipient accepts or rejects. The server checks identity, distance, availability, request rate, pair cooldown, and expiry; computes complementary target positions and facing; waits for both participants to report ready; and then starts one synchronized clip. Movement, cancellation, timeout, disconnection, and completion release both actors. The smoke specification uses three browser contexts to check that the offer and consent prompt stay private while the accepted clip is visible to the room.

Trivia makes a result into a shared piece of theatre. The server keeps the answer key off the live client schema, receives answers, scores the round, and broadcasts one ceremony start time. Every browser derives the current beat from elapsed time, allowing the TV reveal, cards, camera movement, and losing-bird ejection to remain aligned without a stream of choreography messages. Reduced motion is handled inside the same timeline. Voice uses WebRTC peer connections, with the server relaying signaling but never carrying audio; spatial gain makes distance audible without letting a faraway friend disappear entirely. The fireplace takes an equally crafted approach: crackles are synthesized by a recursively scheduled, randomized noise process rather than a loop with a seam, and the owner-tuned values were transferred from a browser sound lab into the game.

Rest is also an activity. A bird can nap on a fireside cushion, choose a sleeping posture, remain visible to passersby, and follow a private three-breath sequence into the Dream. The transition uses its waiting time: breathing animation, stars, camera treatment, and background loading progress together while the lounge body remains asleep. The server authorizes Dream entry only for an eligible napping player, owns one active Dream per room, mints the shared nonzero world seed, and tracks membership. Remote flight is sent as compact validated messages rather than inserted into the room snapshot, so awake lounge clients do not receive a high-rate position stream.

The Dream changes the scale without abandoning the product’s social premise. Each browser advances its own bird through a fixed-step, dependency-light six-degree-of-freedom model. Pitch, roll, wing shape, and discrete flaps feed lift, induced and parasitic drag, gravity, momentum, and coordinated turning. Diving converts altitude into speed; pulling out redirects speed into climb; banking reduces vertical lift. Keyboard, touch, gamepad, and scripted probes resolve to the same control structure. Remote clients reconstruct a partner from motion packets with bounded extrapolation and correction against the same deterministic world.

The world itself is built from public-domain USGS 3DEP elevation data. A 900-metre terrain window is processed and extended into an approximately 3.8-by-8.8-kilometre range. Seeded builders create repeatable rivers, banks, floating landforms, shore, vegetation, and detail. A six-part cover budget divides each point among canopy, shrub, herb, grass, rock, and soil; the same normalized shares drive both ground color and planted geometry, preventing “forest-colored” ground with no forest. A committed species kit varies silhouettes through proportion, while level of detail, culling, tiling, and concentrated detail pockets keep the browser workload compatible with flight-scale terrain.

## Architecture and implementation decisions

Birb Hangout is a TypeScript monorepo. The React 18/Vite client uses React Three Fiber, Three.js helpers, Rapier physics, and Zustand. The Colyseus server uses Express for health, ICE configuration, photo and room-management routes; Colyseus schemas for authoritative room state; and WebSocket messages for commands and transient streams. A shared package defines domain types, protocol payloads, constants, trivia content, and state defaults. The production Docker image builds all three workspaces and serves the compiled client from the same server origin, reducing deployment and CORS complexity.

The state model is intentionally mixed rather than universally synchronized. Durable facts use typed schemas and room records. High-rate Dream flight uses transient relays. Video playback uses authoritative intent plus local browser players. Voice media stays peer-to-peer. A ceremony uses one timestamp and deterministic local playback. A hug uses a server-owned request/staging/lifecycle protocol. This is a strong realtime design choice: each behavior uses the cheapest representation that still preserves the social invariant people notice.

Persistence supports local disk in development and S3-compatible object storage when configured. The same abstraction stores room records and private photo assets. Uploaded images require a short-lived bearer token issued only to a live room session, are limited by type and byte size, decoded under an input-pixel ceiling, autorotated, resized, re-encoded to WebP, and checked against a room quota. Room creation uses production-only per-device, per-network, and global budgets with keyed hashes rather than durable raw IP logs. WebSocket payloads are capped, message types are validated, and invalid-message counts can disconnect abusive clients. These controls do not make a production-security certification claim, but they show that the deployment was treated as a shared service rather than an unconstrained local demo.

## Creative-engineering methodology

The most distinctive contribution may be the way the project learned to build and evaluate subjective 3D work. Visual quality is treated as an observable outcome with human authority, not something a passing typecheck can certify. The repository requires reference-led visual direction, before/after comparison, preserved rejected candidates, and owner sign-off in high-risk lanes. A single clearly defective frame can veto a candidate; a clean frame is not sufficient by itself. Tests are described by what they can establish—transport, timing, schema refusal, deterministic geometry—not promoted into claims about whether an animation feels alive or a landscape looks right.

Animation research produced concrete measurements before a broad retuning effort. Across the original twelve clips, every clip showed a roughly 1.3-to-1.5-world-unit boundary jump over about two frames; six contained dead holds, including several approximately 99 percent frozen clips; and nine lacked anticipation. The instrumented hug experiment preregistered eight likely failures and observed seven of them even though most had been predicted. The lesson was not that animation craft was mysterious. It was that guessed numbers, incomplete rig assumptions, and gates measuring the wrong construct were being allowed to survive.

The resulting candidate system turns iteration into a controlled comparison. A dev-only harness applies frame-keyed motion to an allowlisted set of rig controls, supports linear, cubic-Hermite, and hold interpolation, runs the same A/B/C structure for turntable clips and prop interactions, and restores the baseline atomically. Candidates must differ by mechanism, include an ablation and a reference-derived option, and report only metrics with a registered control. Exact-time captures, named action-axis cameras, orbit views, frame strips, telemetry sidecars, and manifests make runs reproducible.

The team then discovered that even a mechanically correct candidate packet could answer the wrong visual question. A book-pickup packet proved a bookshelf patch while the meaningful book remained merged with the wing. A switch animation showed a static handshake pose beside a self-moving switch. Those failures led to blind-first semantic review: an observer describes what action appears to happen before seeing the intended action; the contract names meaning-carrying cues, ordered events, causal links, counterfactual questions, and disconfirming conditions; only then is the observation compared with the intent. An applied transform is explicitly labeled transport evidence, not semantic completion. Owner uncertainty is handed to the owner instead of being converted into an agent rejection.

Specialist studios reduce each investigation to the smallest truthful environment. Birb Studio isolates the shipped avatar and emotes. Interaction Studio stages real actors and props. Room Studio exposes spatial dependencies. Rig Studio runs the shipped bird, Dream placeholder, and candidate on one baked timeline so model comparisons do not drift out of phase. Dream and Landscape studios expose flight, camera, terrain, species, materials, and one-at-a-time landscape assets. These are not mockups: they import the game’s components, pose functions, builders, and cameras behind development-only routes.

Spatial annotation closes the loop between human visual judgment and AI-assisted implementation. When the owner pauses a studio, orbits and zooms to the failing view, draws around the defect, and writes a comment, the overlay raycasts an 11-by-11 grid through the marked region. Geometry-derived subject IDs compete by vote; ambiguity and hit rate are retained rather than hidden. The durable note stores the frame hash, normalized drawing, camera pose and target, scene state, likely subject, alternate hits, world coordinates, subject-local coordinates, and the instruction needed to reopen the view. A later recapture uses the same camera and state. This converts “the cliff looks broken from this angle” into a bounded work item an AI collaborator can reproduce and compare.

The broader harness reinforces that discipline. Change ledgers preserve the owner request, affected lane, initial red tests, write set, and intentional omissions. Candidate safeguards freeze allowed and protected paths, references, metrics, roles, failure classes, and transition authority in a hash-chained state machine. Rejected mechanisms cannot quietly return under a new name; repeated failure in one class forces an owner decision. Visual review requires an independent unprimed observation, refuses byte-identical variant frames, carries open defects forward by the view where they were seen, and will not accept an after image without its before. The full research compendium documents these systems as evidence rather than retrospective mythology: character, flight, landscape, presence, activities, harness, and studios each separate implemented capability, measured result, human judgment, and held claim.

## Results, significance, and beneficiaries

The strongest result is a coherent product across unusually different modes. The same identity can walk into a room, manipulate shared objects, read privately while remaining socially visible, negotiate media control, join a minigame, share a consensual hug, sleep, and enter a shared flight space. Server state, local presentation, and transient realtime streams remain distinct while still producing one legible experience.

The repository also records evidence of methodological improvement. Phase 2’s structured animation infrastructure passed its recorded 11/11 initial self-test groups, 8/8 focused browser checks, 5/5 metric groups, 15/15 motion tests, and TypeScript checks before owner acceptance. After semantic-review remediation, the recorded candidate self-tests reached 34/34 groups and the focused browser suite 9/9. The owner rejected the first causal reading of the switch interaction, selected the corrected runtime-motion mechanism, and explicitly closed Phase 2. These are historical, commit-scoped results, not a claim that every current test passes.

The current source inventory contains 60 Playwright smoke-spec files and 199 top-level `test(...)` declarations spanning room lifecycle, media synchronization, reading, trivia, voice, hug privacy and synchronization, Dream sharing, flight motion, studios, HUD, mobile behavior, security boundaries, persistence, and visual workflows. That count describes the present test surface; this research pass intentionally did not run it because the source checkout contains extensive owner work in progress.

For players, the work reduces the pressure and fragmentation of remote socializing. For a family member who does not want a conventional game, the room offers recognizable rituals and quiet participation. For a more active player, the Dream offers skill, exploration, and shared landmarks. For designers, the project shows how diegetic objects can advertise activity and invite participation. For realtime engineers, it provides concrete patterns for matching state representation to behavior. For AI-assisted creative teams, its studios and annotation workflow reduce the expensive cycle of describing, misunderstanding, and rediscovering camera-dependent defects.

## Claim ledger

### Measured or recorded results

- The original twelve animation clips were instrumented; the accepted research record reports 1.3–1.5 world-unit boundary jumps across all twelve, dead holds in six, and no anticipation in nine.
- Seven of eight preregistered hug-experiment failure predictions were observed, motivating changes to the workflow rather than cosmetic retuning.
- Recorded Phase 2 verification includes 11/11 candidate self-test groups, 8/8 focused browser checks, 5/5 metric groups, and 15/15 motion checks; the semantic remediation later records 34/34 candidate groups and 9/9 focused browser checks.
- The current checkout contains 60 Playwright smoke-spec files and 199 top-level test declarations. This is an inventory, not a current pass count.
- Reachable Git history attributes 305 of 307 commits to Neerav’s two author identities and two to Claude Code.

### Directly verified capabilities

- Code-based room creation/join/rejoin, owner-token recovery, typed replicated room state, persisted records, shared objects, photo processing, media control, reading, trivia, voice signaling, consent-based hugs, naps, Dream membership, and shared-flight relays are implemented in source.
- The lounge provides world-visible records, books, television, seating, fireplace, gestures, and game ceremonies instead of reducing these activities to disconnected full-screen interfaces.
- The Dream uses a fixed-step flight model, shared seeded terrain, compact remote-flight messages, a USGS-derived heightfield, deterministic landscape builders, and a six-cover vegetation/ground model.
- Development-only studios, exact-time capture, candidate comparison, visual review requests, change ledgers, hash-chained safeguards, and spatial notes with raycast-derived subject and coordinate data are implemented.
- The repository has a single-service Docker/Render deployment design with optional S3 persistence and production-oriented rate, payload, image, and room limits.

### Interpretation

- Birb Hangout demonstrates that a social world can optimize for co-presence rather than retention pressure, continuous conversation, or competition.
- Its technical contribution is the integration of diegetic activity design, fit-for-purpose realtime synchronization, and reproducible human-in-the-loop 3D evaluation.
- The studios and spatial annotation system are a meaningful automation improvement: they turn subjective, viewpoint-dependent feedback into a durable, reopenable, comparable engineering artifact.
- The project is strong evidence of product thinking, realtime full-stack engineering, interaction design, procedural 3D work, evaluation design, and AI-collaboration methodology. It does not by itself establish large-scale retention, population-level social benefit, high-concurrency capacity, or universal visual acceptance.
