AI safety & evaluation workspace

TraceLens

General-purpose chatbots miss emergencies or do not clearly state them to the patient. Can a supporting model both reduce under-triage and make failures transparent to the end user?

A self-built workspace that tests whether a supporting model can catch the emergencies a chatbot misses and make each failure visible: an independent emergency detector, a disclosed reconsideration loop, a preregistered study on physician-labelled cases, and a cascade decision-model architecture.

Skills applied
  • LLM Evaluation
  • Harness Engineering
  • Python
  • PyTorch
  • Vega reached held-out AUC 0.966: it ranks a true emergency above a non-emergency 97% of the time, accurate enough for its flags to be acted on. It caught 18 of the chatbot's 31 missed emergencies and disclosed each flag, raising final emergency guidance from 39 to 47 of 70 with one added false alarm in 134 non-emergencies.
  • Vega out-ranked the chatbot's own pre-answer emergency likelihood (AUC 0.92 vs 0.76), so the independent reader of the message is the stronger safety signal.
  • A preregistered held-out study confirmed the problem: with a plain prompt, Gemma 3 4B gave 15 of 39 physician-labelled emergencies less than emergency care; a commit-first prompt raised emergency guidance to 35/39 (McNemar p = 0.003) with 18 false alarms in 77.

Interpretable MRI atrophy measurement

Brain Atrophy Analysis in Dementia

Published in the IEEE ICRITO 2022 proceedings, the research operationalizes visible signs of cerebral atrophy as explicit OpenCV measurements. The resulting pipeline converts color masks into an auditable score, compares that score with reference bounds derived from 2,198 ADNI images, and tests how anatomical focus changes classification performance.

Skills applied
  • Python
  • OpenCV
  • Medical Imaging
  • Interpretable CV
  • Analyzes 2,198 ADNI T1-weighted MRI images: 1,172 CDR 0 images and 1,026 images spanning CDR 0.5–2.
  • Defines the interpretable dem ratio as dark-region density divided by combined white- and grey-region density, connecting every score to visible pixel counts.
  • Raises reported classification accuracy from 66% with the default crop to 71% with a hippocampal crop, a five-percentage-point improvement.

Four-class MRI classification and ensemble learning

Four-Class Alzheimer MRI Classification

The study connects data inspection, performant TensorFlow ingestion, training-only augmentation, two CNN scales, reusable inference, image-level error review, checkpoint reuse, and two- and three-model probability ensembles inside one inspectable Colab workflow.

Skills applied
  • Python
  • TensorFlow
  • Keras
  • Deep Learning
  • Processes 6,400 MRI files across NonDemented, VeryMildDemented, MildDemented, and ModerateDemented classes with a recorded 5,120/640/640-image split.
  • Compares a 2.11-million-parameter six-block CNN with a saved 491,524-parameter variant, then composes independently trained checkpoints into common evaluable graphs.
  • Reports a seven-percentage-point held-out improvement in the companion experiment, from a 78% single-model baseline to an 85% ensemble result.
